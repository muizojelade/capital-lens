Markets rarely move in a straight line.

Periods of optimism can eventually give way to uncertainty. Strong economic growth can slow. Fear can eventually turn into confidence.

These recurring changes are commonly described as **market cycles**.

Understanding cycles does not allow investors to predict the future perfectly, but it provides a useful framework for interpreting changing market conditions.

## The Expansion Phase

A typical cycle often begins with improving economic conditions.

Businesses may experience stronger demand, employment can improve, and corporate earnings may begin increasing.

As confidence grows, investors become more willing to take risk.

Asset prices can rise as expectations improve.

This phase can last for years, but eventually the pace of growth may begin to slow.

## The Peak

The peak represents a period when economic activity and investor optimism are strong, but the rate of improvement may begin to weaken.

This stage can be difficult to identify in real time.

Markets may still be rising while underlying growth is losing momentum.

Valuations can also become stretched as investors become increasingly confident.

The important distinction is that a strong economy does not necessarily mean that markets must continue rising at the same pace.

## The Contraction

Eventually, economic activity may begin to weaken.

Companies can experience slower sales, unemployment may increase, and consumers may become more cautious.

Investor sentiment often deteriorates during this stage.

Riskier assets can experience significant declines as investors prioritize capital preservation.

However, falling markets do not automatically mean that long-term opportunities have disappeared.

Markets often begin anticipating recovery before economic data clearly shows it.

## Recovery

The recovery phase begins when economic conditions start improving again.

Confidence gradually returns.

Businesses may increase investment, consumers may become more active, and investors may begin taking on more risk.

This creates the foundation for another expansion.

The process then continues.

## Why Cycles Matter

The value of understanding market cycles is not that they provide an exact timing mechanism.

Instead, cycles help investors recognize the broader environment in which individual assets are operating.

For example, an investment strategy that works well during strong economic growth may behave differently during a recession.

Understanding the environment can therefore help investors manage expectations and risk.

## Cycles Are Not Perfect

Real markets do not follow a perfectly predictable sequence.

Different economies can be at different stages of their cycles.

Unexpected events can also accelerate or interrupt a cycle.

Central-bank policy, technological changes, geopolitical events, financial crises, and consumer behavior can all influence the path.

That is why cycles should be treated as a framework rather than a precise forecasting tool.

## The Bigger Picture

Markets are constantly balancing economic conditions, expectations, valuations, liquidity, and investor psychology.

Market cycles provide a way to organize those changing conditions.

Rather than asking whether markets are simply "bullish" or "bearish," investors can ask a more useful question:

**What stage of the broader economic and market environment are we currently experiencing?**

That question encourages a more patient and analytical approach to investing.
